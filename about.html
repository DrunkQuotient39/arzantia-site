About Arzantia

Born from the cedar

Arzantia takes its name from arz, the Arabic word for cedar, the tree of Lebanon. People have told stories under it, sailed on it and written about it for thousands of years. We built a table worthy of its shade.

Arz+antia=Arzantia

The land of the cedar

Arz is the cedar. -antia is the sound of a place on an old map, the way Atlantis or Arcadia name a land you could sail to if you only knew the way.

Put together, Arzantia is the land of the cedar: a place where stories take root, and where a party can always find its way back to the table.

Four rings of the cedar

A tree that has always been in the story

A cedar grows slowly and keeps a ring for every year. Read from the heart outwards, these four rings tell why we named a game table after it.

IThe first quest
Ring I

The first quest

The oldest adventure story we know, the Epic of Gilgamesh, sends two companions down a long road to a forest of cedars, guarded by a monster.

About four thousand years later, the shape still holds. A party, a road, a forest, and something waiting among the trees.

Ring II

Ships and letters

On the coast of what is now Lebanon, the Phoenicians built their ships from cedar and sailed the Mediterranean, steering by the stars.

They carried something lighter than timber: an alphabet. Greek and Latin letters grew from it, and so did the A in our mark. Every story written down since owes them a little.

Ring III

The grove that stays

High on Mount Lebanon, in the grove called the Cedars of God, trees stand in the snow that have outlived kingdoms. Some are more than a thousand years old.

The cedar is on Lebanon's flag, and it means what it always has: endurance, shelter, and a place to come back to.

Ring IV

Your table

That's what we want Arzantia to be: a place your group comes back to, week after week.

It's a table under the branches, where a campaign can grow slowly, ring by ring, into something that lasts.

What the cedar taught us

Four things we build by

Deep roots

Your scenes, tokens, notes and battles stay with your campaign. Your world is yours, and it stays where you planted it.

Wide shelter

Everyone fits under the branches: the DM at the screen, the players on their phones, the whole room at the TV.

Slow growth

Good campaigns take years. The Battle Chronicle, the notes and the quest journal keep every ring of yours.

Far reach

Cedar crossed the sea. Your table can too: a private link brings in friends from anywhere.
